# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Free-software communities, nonprofits, public-sector teams, and self-hosters that care about open governance.
- Organizations that want a Gitea-like product but prefer Codeberg and Forgejo's community trust model.
- Sovereignty-motivated buyers reducing dependence on GitHub, GitLab.com, or other commercial SaaS for source code.
- Instance operators interested in future forge federation.

## Positioning

Forgejo positions itself as a painless, self-hosted Git service, but the broader market signal is "community-governed forge." Codeberg positions its hosted service as a democratic, non-profit, privacy-friendly alternative to commercial services, and Forgejo benefits from that association.

## Customer Model

Forgejo is free software for self-hosting. Codeberg is a hosted non-profit service built on Forgejo. Public-sector and community deployments use Forgejo as infrastructure they can study, modify, and operate themselves.

## Captures

- Users who would otherwise choose Gitea but distrust commercial governance.
- Public-interest organizations that need code infrastructure to be auditable and not controlled by a US platform vendor.
- Free-software contributors who value federation, privacy, and community ownership as product requirements.

## Misses

- Enterprises that want a single vendor contract, broad compliance package, and mature sales/support motion may choose GitLab or Gitea Enterprise.
- Teams requiring stable cross-instance federation today may find Forgejo's roadmap too early.
- Developers who prioritize the GitHub contributor network may not trade reach for sovereignty.

## Epoch Lessons

Epoch's marketing should separate cryptographic portability from governance preference. Forgejo can win trust as an institution; Epoch needs to win trust with verifiable data structures that still work inside or outside Forgejo.
