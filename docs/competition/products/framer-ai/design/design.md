# Design

## Look And Feel

Framer's AI surface inherits a highly visual design-tool language: breakpoints, canvas previews, page sections, component properties, generated pages, AI prompt suggestions, localization progress, plugins, and publish-ready site controls. It feels designer-native first, developer-extensible second.

## Open Design Artifacts

- Public pages show AI Wireframer prompts, generated responsive pages, Workshop component examples, AI Translate progress, AI plugin examples, and site navigation.
- Framer exposes brand guidelines, templates, components, plugins, marketplace assets, and developer documentation.
- Pricing pages expose plan-level constraints around pages, CMS, bandwidth, localization, hosting, roles, staging, rollback, and enterprise support.

## Differentiators

- Framer's AI features sit inside a mature visual website builder rather than a standalone chat workspace.
- Wireframer, Workshop, AI Translate, CMS, hosting, and on-page editing make AI part of a full marketing-site operations loop.
- The platform preserves designer control while letting non-designers make safer content edits.

## What Works Well

- Designers can start from AI without leaving a professional canvas and publishing platform.
- Marketers and content editors can contribute through on-page editing and CMS workflows.
- Built-in SEO, hosting, localization, rollback, and performance features reduce handoff overhead for marketing sites.

## Where It Breaks Down

- Framer is strongest for websites and content-heavy surfaces; complex application logic still belongs elsewhere.
- AI-generated sites can look polished but generic if teams do not invest in brand direction and design taste.
- Hosting convenience can hide portability, source-control, and provenance gaps for organizations that require repository-first governance.
