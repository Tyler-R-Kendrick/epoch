# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Non-specialist builders, founders, students, and small teams who want to turn an idea into a running app quickly.
- Developers who value a cloud workspace with integrated hosting more than local editor control.
- Teams prototyping internal tools, demos, web apps, APIs, and background jobs without setting up infrastructure.

## Positioning

Replit positions Agent as an AI builder and companion for going from idea to software. The pitch is broader than coding assistance: Replit sells the whole loop of prompt, plan, code, run, test, checkpoint, and deploy in one place.

## Customer Model

Replit combines subscriptions, monthly credits, Agent usage, and deployment spend. Publishing can be usage-based or more predictable depending on deployment type, while Agent work consumes credits according to the current plan and usage model.

## Captures

- Users who would otherwise never configure a local toolchain, GitHub repo, database, and hosting provider.
- Rapid prototypers who care about seeing a live app more than owning every infrastructure detail.
- Builders who prefer a guided browser workspace to terminal-first coding agents.

## Misses

- Professional teams that need strict auditability, self-hosting, deterministic CI, or deep repository governance.
- Cost-sensitive users who are uncomfortable with credit burn during uncertain agent loops.
- Developers who want to keep source, deployment, and database state portable across tools.

## Epoch Lessons

Epoch should treat Replit as evidence that the market values end-to-end creation over isolated version-control features. Epoch's opportunity is to bring durable trust and portability to that creation loop.
