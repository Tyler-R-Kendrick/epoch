# Bito Marketing

## Target Customer

Bito markets to engineering managers, maintainers, and teams that are producing more AI-assisted pull requests than humans can comfortably review. Its strongest audience is mid-market and enterprise teams that want practical review automation across GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Jira, and coding agents.

## Positioning

- Bito positions itself as the context layer for engineering workflow, not only a pull-request bot.
- The AI Code Review Agent is framed as codebase-aware, customizable, analytics-backed, and connected to existing team tools.
- AI Architect and Jira knowledge graph messaging extends the story from review into planning and technical design.

## Customer Model

- Bito offers plan-based pricing with trials and higher tiers for professional, enterprise, and larger team needs.
- Expansion comes from more repositories, more review volume, custom guidelines, analytics, self-hosting, and organizational context.
- The product is designed to meet teams where they already work rather than replace their coding agent or Git provider.

## Who It Captures

- Teams drowning in AI-generated pull requests and looking for a first review pass before human maintainers spend time.
- Organizations that need repository-specific rules, Jira alignment, and analytics around code review throughput.
- Developers who want AI reviewers to feed findings back into a coding agent for immediate fixes.

## Who It Misses

- Teams skeptical that AI review catches meaningful design or logic defects.
- Organizations that want every review decision stored as signed project history instead of dashboard metrics and PR comments.
- Very small teams that can use cheaper generic code review or built-in coding-agent checks.

## Epoch Opportunity

Epoch should view Bito as a focused review competitor. The opening is end-to-end provenance: connect issue intent, review guidance, AI findings, human acceptance, and final code history in a signed chain.
