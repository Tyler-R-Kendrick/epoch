# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Small teams, homelabs, agencies, and organizations that want self-hosted Git collaboration without GitLab's operational weight.
- Buyers that want open-source code hosting with optional commercial support.
- Teams migrating from GitHub workflows but wanting local control over repositories, CI runners, and packages.
- Enterprises that need SAML SSO, audit logs, priority support, and managed deployment assistance without changing the core forge model.

## Positioning

Gitea positions itself as a painless, lightweight, self-hosted all-in-one software development service. The marketing centers on speed, easy installation, low infrastructure requirements, GitHub Actions-compatible CI/CD, package support, and deployment flexibility.

## Customer Model

Gitea has a free open-source self-hosted core and paid enterprise or cloud-managed offerings. Public pricing presents an open-source self-managed option and paid enterprise support with annual commitment terms, priority support, SAML SSO, audit logs, and enhanced runner capabilities.

## Captures

- Users who want a familiar forge with lower cost and lower operational complexity than GitLab.
- Teams that distrust hosted GitHub but still want GitHub-like pull requests, Actions, and package workflows.
- Self-hosters who value simple upgrades, SQLite/PostgreSQL/MySQL flexibility, and small resource usage.

## Misses

- Teams seeking a governance-neutral community project may prefer Forgejo.
- Enterprise DevSecOps buyers may still choose GitLab for deeper security, compliance, and planning integration.
- Developers who want radically better version-control semantics will not get them from a conventional Git forge.
- Open-source communities that optimize for network effect and contributor discovery still default to GitHub.

## Epoch Lessons

Epoch should meet Gitea users where they are: self-hosted, cost-aware, and skeptical of heavy platforms. The pitch should be "portable signed history that works beside your forge," not "replace your forge first."
