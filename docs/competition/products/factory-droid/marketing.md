# Factory Droid Marketing

## Target Customer

Factory markets Droid to individual developers who want complete software development agents and to enterprise platform teams that need secure autonomous development across laptops, terminals, CI/CD, VMs, Kubernetes, hardened devcontainers, and airgapped environments.

## Positioning

- "Agent-native software development platform" is the core category.
- Droid is positioned as multi-surface and infrastructure-friendly rather than tied to one cloud IDE.
- Enterprise positioning emphasizes the highest-security customers: banks, governments, healthcare, national security, and regulated organizations.

## Customer Model

- Pro starts at $20 per month for individual agent-native development.
- Plus and Max increase rolling usage limits and add access to managed Droid Computers or early features.
- Teams and Enterprise move to custom pricing with SSO, SCIM, model controls, audit logging, data residency, network policy, deployment options, and support.
- Extra Usage and Droid Core create a cost fallback when standard usage is exhausted.

## Who It Captures

- Developers who want a dedicated coding-agent platform outside a general chat product.
- Teams that need local, cloud, CI, and enterprise deployment flexibility.
- Platform operators who need agent telemetry, policy hierarchy, and compliance narratives.

## Who It Misses

- Small teams that want a simple GitHub-label async agent with minimal setup.
- Buyers who prefer open-source, repository-native primitives over a vendor agent platform.
- Developers who find rolling token and compute limits too complex for day-to-day planning.

## Epoch Opportunity

Factory shows that enterprise buyers want readiness, policy, and telemetry. Epoch should make those benefits repository-native by signing the actual history and evidence that agents create.
