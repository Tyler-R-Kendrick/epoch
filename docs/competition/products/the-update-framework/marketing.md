# The Update Framework Marketing

## Target Customer

TUF primarily targets package repository operators, Linux distribution maintainers, container and artifact registry teams, security architects, and platform engineers who own software update integrity. It is less aimed at individual application developers and more at organizations that distribute software to many clients.

## Positioning

The positioning is framework-first: TUF is a security standard for protecting update systems against compromised mirrors, rollback attacks, freeze attacks, key compromise, and inconsistent metadata. It sells confidence in distribution, not developer productivity.

## Customer Model

- Open standard and open-source implementation ecosystem.
- Adoption usually happens through a package manager, registry, signing system, or internal platform rather than a direct TUF purchase.
- Commercial value accrues to integrators and vendors that build hosted signing, repository, or package-distribution products around the model.

## Who They Capture

- Security-conscious infrastructure teams that need a recognized update-security model.
- Ecosystem maintainers who need a shared protocol across clients and repositories.
- Buyers who prefer standards-backed controls over single-vendor artifact trust.

## Who They Do Not Capture

- Teams looking for a complete collaboration UI, hosted source-control workflow, or review experience.
- Product teams unwilling to operate key ceremonies and metadata publishing pipelines.
- Developers who need friendly local history, merge, review, and provenance explanations in one workspace.

## Differentiation Notes

TUF differentiates through the depth of its threat model and its maturity as a standard. Epoch can differentiate beside it by making the source-history side of the trust chain more understandable, local, and human-auditable before update metadata is emitted.
