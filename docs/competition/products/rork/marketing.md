# Marketing

## Target Customer

Rork markets to app founders, solo builders, and mobile-first entrepreneurs who want to create and monetize native apps without hiring a traditional mobile team.

## Positioning

- Rork positions itself as a way to create mobile apps by chatting with AI, ship to app stores, and start making money.
- The docs frame the product around the full founder path: validating an idea, prompting well, testing on devices, adding paywalls, creating legal pages, publishing, and marketing.
- Rork Max is positioned as the more advanced path for native iOS app creation.

## Customer Model

- Public pages expose a free prompt entry path and upgrade cues around larger file uploads.
- The product appears to monetize through usage/subscription upgrades and a higher-capability Max tier.
- Founder-focused education and affiliate links suggest acquisition through creator, app-founder, and indie-hacker channels.

## Who They Capture

- Builders who want app-store outcomes rather than only web previews.
- Non-mobile developers who find Xcode, Android tooling, certificates, and store setup expensive or confusing.
- Founders who care about paywalls, trials, app marketing, and revenue quickly.

## Who They Miss

- Teams that need enterprise mobile release governance, signed build provenance, device-lab evidence, or regulated privacy review before store submission.
- Developers who prefer explicit React Native, Expo, Swift, or Kotlin control from the first commit.
- Organizations that need transparent pricing and contractual controls before uploading app assets.

## Epoch Takeaway

Rork's mobile-first founder funnel is a reminder that Epoch should model release channels, store metadata, monetization decisions, and mobile build provenance as product history, not just source-code commits.
