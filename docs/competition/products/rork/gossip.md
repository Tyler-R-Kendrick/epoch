# Gossip

## Positive Sentiment

- Rork's public story is compelling because it makes mobile launch feel reachable to solo founders.
- The focus on app-store publishing and monetization is sharper than many prompt-to-web-app builders.
- Browser-only creation removes a major barrier for users who have never configured a mobile development environment.

## Complaints And Friction

- The public site emphasizes founder success and speed more than detailed technical guarantees, which can leave cautious teams asking how builds, signing, code ownership, and app-store review are proven.
- Mobile AI generation inherits the usual app-builder risk: early screens can look convincing before edge cases, offline behavior, notifications, accessibility, privacy, and store-compliance work is complete.
- Pricing and tier boundaries were difficult to verify from public crawlable content in this run.

## Bugs And Product Risk

- App-store launch is a high-friction external dependency; generated code can be blocked by review, privacy, entitlement, or payment-policy issues.
- Monetization integrations such as paywalls and free trials require careful state, entitlement, and restore-purchase behavior.
- Backend and API integrations raise data-security questions that a mobile founder may not be equipped to audit.

## Epoch Opportunity

Epoch can compete by preserving mobile-app generation and release evidence: generated code, build inputs, signing state, store metadata, paywall configuration, and review outcomes should be replayable and inspectable.
