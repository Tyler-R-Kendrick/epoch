# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Game studios, VFX teams, semiconductor companies, automotive teams, and enterprises with large binary or design assets.
- Organizations that need centralized control, granular permissions, audit trails, global performance, and professional support.
- Mixed creative and engineering teams where artists, producers, and developers need different interfaces over the same versioned assets.
- Smaller game or creative teams that can enter through the free tier or P4 Cloud.

## Positioning

Perforce positions P4 as version control that handles what other systems cannot: huge files, millions of assets, global scale, protected IP, and collision avoidance. The marketing explicitly contrasts P4 with Git for asset-heavy teams while also offering a Git connector for developers who need familiar tools.

## Customer Model

- Free plan for up to 5 users and 20 workspaces, with limits for larger usage.
- P4 Cloud at a published per-user monthly price for managed teams under 100 users.
- Sales-led Scale and Platform plans for unlimited users, enterprise support, add-ons, DAM, planning, and advanced deployments.
- Service revenue from administration, migration, upgrade, audits, health checks, and integrations.

## Captured Audiences

- AAA and professional studios that already view Perforce as the industry default.
- Enterprise buyers who need vendor accountability and deployment support.
- Teams with binary assets where merge-based distributed workflows fail.
- Organizations that want one source of truth for code, design assets, and IP.

## Missed Or Under-Captured Audiences

- Small developer teams that prefer GitHub-native workflows and do not want a centralized server model.
- Open-source communities where decentralized contribution and low-friction public collaboration matter.
- Local-first application builders who want embedded sync rather than enterprise VCS infrastructure.
- Teams that need transparent open-source internals or low-cost self-service beyond the free tier.
