# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Developers building local-first, offline-capable, multiplayer applications.
- Product teams that want Google Docs-style collaboration without making a central server the sole source of truth.
- Infrastructure-minded teams that need a CRDT engine they can embed in their own app and sync topology.
- Open-source and research-adjacent builders who value transparent algorithms and portable data.

## Positioning

Automerge positions itself as a sync engine and version-control layer for user data. Its public message emphasizes local-first behavior: work offline, edit instantly, merge consistently, preserve history, and synchronize across devices or collaborators.

## Customer Model

- Open-source core project and JavaScript ecosystem packages.
- Adoption through developer education, examples, docs, and community trust rather than a traditional enterprise sales funnel.
- Hosted or managed product capture is not the primary public motion; the project captures mindshare by becoming the embedded sync substrate inside applications.

## Captured Audiences

- Teams that see centralized SaaS collaboration as too fragile, slow, or vendor-controlled.
- Developers who want collaboration without designing merge algorithms from scratch.
- Browser and cross-device app builders who need IndexedDB and WebSocket-style examples.
- Engineers already convinced by local-first software principles.

## Missed Or Under-Captured Audiences

- Teams that need a ready-made repository UI, pull request workflow, permission console, or compliance reporting.
- Non-developer users looking for a packaged collaborative editor or document product.
- Enterprises that require clear vendor support, hosted admin controls, SSO, and procurement packaging.
- Source-control teams that need Git/forge migration paths rather than an application data library.
