# Google Antigravity Marketing

## Target Customer

Antigravity markets to developers and technical builders who want to operate at a task-oriented level with autonomous agents. Google also targets existing Google AI subscribers and Gemini users who can adopt a coding platform as part of a broader AI productivity subscription.

## Positioning

The positioning is agent-first development: developers manage agents across workspaces while retaining familiar IDE workflows when needed. Antigravity 2.0 sharpens that positioning by treating the agent manager as a distinct application and by adding a CLI for terminal-first delegation.

## Customer Model

- Google AI subscription and usage limits shape adoption.
- CLI, IDE, and desktop app surfaces create multiple entry points.
- Gemini model access and Google ecosystem integration are the strategic moat.
- Usage and quota management are part of the customer experience, even when framed through subscription tiers.

## Who It Captures

- Developers who want Google's Gemini-backed coding workflow.
- Users who want multi-agent supervision rather than single-assistant chat.
- Builders who trust Google's platform distribution and model roadmap.
- Teams exploring browser-using coding agents and artifact-based review.

## Who It Does Not Capture

- Users who need fully transparent, repository-local trust boundaries.
- Developers frustrated by subscription quotas or unclear credit consumption.
- Security-sensitive users who require strict confirmation before terminal actions.
- Maintainers who prefer open-source assistants and self-managed model routing.

## Marketing Strengths and Weaknesses

The strongest marketing move is category creation: Antigravity tries to define agent-first development before the category stabilizes. The weakness is that the same pitch raises the bar for safety, quotas, and user control; when users feel agents are hidden or expensive, the product promise becomes a liability.
