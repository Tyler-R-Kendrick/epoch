# Sentry Seer Marketing

## Target Customer

Sentry markets Seer to engineering teams already using Sentry for errors, performance, logs, tracing, releases, and code ownership. The best-fit customer wants AI assistance grounded in production evidence rather than a generic code reviewer.

## Positioning

- Seer is positioned as an AI debugger that can root-cause issues, suggest fixes, open pull requests, and now help during local development and code review.
- The proof story emphasizes Sentry's telemetry context and claimed root-cause/fix productivity metrics.
- Code review is marketed as part of the broader debugging lifecycle, not a standalone PR-comment product.

## Customer Model

- Seer is available to paid Sentry customers and billed separately from normal pay-as-you-go budgets.
- Pricing docs describe a flat monthly active-contributor charge based on contributors opening at least two PRs in Seer-connected repositories.
- Buyers are likely engineering leaders, SREs, and platform teams already paying for observability.

## Who They Capture

- Teams with rich Sentry telemetry and clear code mappings.
- Organizations that want AI fixes tied to real incidents and performance regressions.
- Engineering managers who prefer predictable active-contributor billing over per-review token pricing.

## Who They Miss

- Teams not using Sentry or not instrumenting enough runtime context.
- Organizations that need AI review before any telemetry exists.
- Users who want every review and fix artifact to remain forge-native and vendor-portable.
