# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Developers who want an AI-first IDE with a lower-friction side-panel agent experience than traditional extensions.
- Teams that value repeatable workflows, shared Cascade trajectories, enterprise rules, and model choice.
- Startups and product engineers who want AI coding plus quick preview deployment without leaving the editor.

## Positioning

Windsurf positions itself around "flow": an IDE where AI understands the current workspace and collaborates continuously. The Cascade name is the center of the brand, and the docs frame coding as a trajectory with plans, queued messages, tools, memories, workflows, and deploys.

## Customer Model

Windsurf uses self-serve and enterprise plans with quota or ACU-style model accounting, BYOK options, team controls, and enterprise customization. The market narrative also includes neutrality across model providers after the acquisition turbulence around OpenAI, Google, and Cognition.

## Captures

- Users who find Cursor expensive or overly tied to a single product direction.
- Developers who want richer agent workflows but still prefer a GUI IDE.
- Enterprises that want centrally enforced workflows, ignore rules, and managed model access.

## Misses

- Terminal-first users who prefer CLI agents and explicit Git operations.
- Teams that need stable governance guarantees more than fast-moving AI IDE features.
- Buyers wary of ownership churn, quota changes, and dependency on external model providers.

## Epoch Lessons

Epoch should position itself below the AI IDE competition. Windsurf can own the agent interaction; Epoch can own durable signed history, workflow provenance, and deployment evidence.
