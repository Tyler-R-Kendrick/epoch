# Marketing

## Target Customer

Magic Patterns markets to product teams, product managers, designers, engineers, marketers, sales teams, founders, and enterprise teams that need fast, on-brand prototypes.

## Positioning

- It positions itself as the AI design tool for product teams to discover features, create prototypes, gather feedback, and move toward production.
- The strongest claim is design-system matching: prototypes should look like the existing product rather than generic AI UI.
- Enterprise positioning emphasizes secure, compliant prototyping with SOC 2 and ISO 27001 claims.

## Customer Model

- The homepage describes a limited free tier, Starter at $20 per seat per month, Business at $100 per seat per month, and custom Enterprise pricing.
- Collaboration, design-system import, security, and team workflows are the higher-value levers.
- The product uses community-generated examples and customer logos to prove breadth and adoption.

## Who They Capture

- PMs and designers who need quick customer-testable flows before engineering investment.
- Enterprises that already have design systems and want AI generation to respect them.
- Engineers who want prototype context through handoff artifacts or MCP instead of ambiguous screenshots.

## Who They Miss

- Teams that need production code, tests, data models, and deployment evidence from the same tool.
- Organizations that treat prototypes as insufficient unless tied directly to requirements, Gherkin scenarios, and source-control history.
- Builders who want a complete app runtime rather than design and handoff.

## Epoch Takeaway

Magic Patterns makes product intent more concrete before code. Epoch should connect that intent to durable acceptance criteria, implementation changes, and evidence so prototypes do not become orphaned design artifacts.
