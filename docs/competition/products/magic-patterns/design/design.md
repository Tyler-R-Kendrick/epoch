# Design

## Look And Feel

Magic Patterns presents as a polished product-team canvas rather than a developer IDE. The homepage foregrounds prompt examples, customer logos, generated design galleries, design-system matching, collaboration, and enterprise trust.

## Open Design Artifacts

- Public pages show prototype examples, product-team role copy, generated community designs, and design-system matching claims.
- Docs describe a canvas, templates, Figma import, team workspaces, published previews, custom domains, password protection, and an MCP server for engineering workflows.
- The product's key design artifact is the existing customer design system: Magic Patterns claims to copy an app's styling and keep prototypes consistent with real components.

## Differentiators

- Magic Patterns is aimed at product discovery and customer feedback, not just code scaffolding.
- Design-system import and matching are central to the pitch, making fidelity a first-order product promise.
- Real-time multiplayer gives design and engineering a shared review surface before work becomes a ticket or pull request.

## What Works Well

- The workflow fits PM and design behavior: explore variants, test flows, share previews, and gather feedback quickly.
- Enterprise trust claims reduce friction for teams that cannot paste product screenshots into arbitrary AI tools.
- Engineering handoff and MCP positioning make the prototype more useful as agent context.

## Where It Breaks Down

- Prototype fidelity can create false confidence if generated screens are not tied to implementation constraints, data contracts, or acceptance tests.
- Design-system matching depends on the quality and completeness of the imported system.
- The product may miss teams that want repository-native proof before treating a prototype as committed scope.
