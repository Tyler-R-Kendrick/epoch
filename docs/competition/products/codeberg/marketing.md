# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Open-source maintainers who want hosted Git collaboration without GitHub's corporate ownership model.
- Free-software communities that care about nonprofit governance, transparency, and donation-backed infrastructure.
- Projects that want a ready hosted Forgejo experience instead of operating their own instance.

## Positioning

Codeberg positions itself as a community-run home for free and open-source projects. Its marketing emphasizes independence, nonprofit governance, free use for open projects, and the ability to contribute back through donations and association participation.

## Customer Model

The customer model is not classic SaaS conversion. Codeberg is sustained through donations, membership, and community support. That model captures values-aligned projects but may not satisfy organizations that expect enterprise procurement, custom contracts, and formal support guarantees.

## Captures

- Maintainers who want to leave GitHub for governance reasons but still need hosted convenience.
- Communities that value public-good infrastructure and can tolerate a smaller ecosystem.
- Projects whose workflows fit standard Git forge features.

## Misses

- Enterprise buyers seeking commercial SLAs, compliance packages, and account management.
- Teams that depend heavily on GitHub Actions marketplace integrations, Copilot, Sponsors, or social discovery.
- Users looking for new version-control semantics rather than different hosting governance.

## Epoch Lessons

Epoch can learn from Codeberg's governance clarity. A technical trust story will land better if the hosting and community model are equally explicit.
