# Design

## Look And Feel

Pijul's design is minimalist and documentation-first. The site uses simple pages, sparse typography, and theory-forward explanations. There is little visual product UI; the core experience is CLI and manual.

## Open Design Assets

- Official website and manual.
- Nest-hosted repository and discussion pages.
- CLI examples in documentation.

## Differentiators

- The product design centers the theory: commutation, merge correctness, channels, partial clones, and conflicts as modeled changes.
- It avoids SaaS polish and presents itself as a serious systems tool.

## What Works

- The conceptual differentiation is crisp for users dissatisfied with Git's snapshot and rebase model.
- The manual explains conflicts directly instead of hiding them behind happy-path workflows.

## UX Breakdowns

- Sparse visual design and limited onboarding make it harder for mainstream teams to see day-to-day workflow benefits quickly.
- Theory-heavy language can feel abstract before users experience the payoff.
- Lack of a familiar hosted collaboration layer increases switching cost.
