# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Open-source maintainers, hackers, privacy-conscious developers, and communities comfortable with email workflows.
- Users who want Git or Mercurial hosting plus builds, tickets, lists, pages, paste, and manual pages without a mainstream SaaS feel.
- Contributors who value no tracking, no advertising, no AI features, free/open-source software, and JavaScript-free operation.
- Maintainers who prefer CLI/API/scriptable workflows over dashboards.

## Positioning

SourceHut positions itself as "the hacker's forge": efficient engineering tools derived from successful open-source communities. Its marketing is explicitly contrarian against mainstream SaaS complexity and AI-first product trends.

## Customer Model

SourceHut uses paid subscriptions for users who host projects, with equal feature access across price tiers and financial aid for users who cannot comfortably pay. It is also self-hostable free software.

## Captures

- Developers who feel GitHub/GitLab/Atlassian are too heavy, commercial, or tracking-oriented.
- Projects that use mailing lists, patches, and long-lived public archives as their collaboration backbone.
- Users who need Mercurial support alongside Git.
- Buyers who treat exportability and self-hostability as values rather than enterprise checkboxes.

## Misses

- Teams expecting GitHub-style social features, graphical PR review, AI summaries, or rich project dashboards.
- Enterprise buyers looking for a polished sales-led compliance platform.
- Contributors unfamiliar with email patch review who need a lower-friction onboarding path.
- Organizations that require stronger uptime guarantees than a small public-alpha service can signal.

## Epoch Lessons

Epoch can learn from SourceHut's values clarity. It should state which workflows it refuses to optimize for, and it should make export, audit, and low-dependency operation part of the product contract.
