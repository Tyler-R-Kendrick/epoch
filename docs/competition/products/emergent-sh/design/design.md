# Design

## Look And Feel

Emergent presents app building as a conversational, agentic workspace: describe the desired app, let specialized agents assemble the product, inspect the result, iterate, push to GitHub, fork when needed, and deploy when ready. The public community guidance emphasizes a beginner-friendly map of features and pricing rather than a developer-first IDE mental model.

## Open Design Artifacts

- Public product and pricing pages expose the prompt-to-app, credits, hosting, GitHub, and deployment model.
- Community docs and beginner guides describe the main workflow as conversation-driven app generation.
- Third-party reviews highlight full-stack generation, GitHub integration, deployment, and mobile-app aspirations as the visible product promises.

## Differentiators

- Emergent sells a low-learning-curve full-stack app builder experience rather than a code editor.
- GitHub handoff is an important trust signal for users who want the "true code" outside the platform.
- Forking and credit packages create an explicit iteration model for trying alternate paths.
- Mobile-oriented claims separate it from web-only component or landing-page builders.

## What Works Well

- The product is easy for non-coders to understand: describe the app and watch a working product appear.
- GitHub export and deploy stories reduce the sense of lock-in.
- Community examples suggest users can ship simple products and keep iterating without learning a full local stack first.

## Where It Breaks Down

- Conversation-first design can hide what the agent changed, tested, skipped, or charged.
- Deployment and mobile claims are hard for users to verify until late in the process.
- Credit use during failed tests or deploy attempts creates a strong negative experience.
- Users who need precise debugging often end up wanting a normal repository, local tools, and a stronger model outside the platform.
