# Marketing

## Target Customer

Cline targets developers who want open-source, provider-flexible coding agents inside their existing editor or terminal, plus enterprises that want coding-agent adoption without surrendering code, prompts, model routing, or governance to a closed IDE vendor.

## Positioning

- Cline positions itself around transparency, user approval, any-model provider choice, local/client-side processing, open source, and usage-based inference.
- The developer story emphasizes Plan/Act, checkpoints, terminal commands, browser use, MCP tools, and visible token/cost behavior.
- The enterprise story emphasizes SSO, RBAC, model/tool controls, centralized billing, remote configuration, observability, VPC/on-prem/air-gapped deployment, and team dashboards.

## Customer Model

- Individual use is free as open source; users pay for inference through BYOK or Cline provider credits.
- Enterprise is custom-priced and adds governance, support, team management, JetBrains extension access, central billing, audit logs, and provider restrictions.
- The model avoids fixed subscription margins for solo developers while monetizing organizational control and support.

## Who They Capture

- VS Code, Cursor, Windsurf, JetBrains, terminal, and Zed users who want a shared open-source agent layer.
- Developers who dislike subscription limits and want direct provider choice.
- Enterprises that need governance, provider control, and local processing claims before adopting AI coding agents.

## Who They Miss

- Teams that prefer a single bundled IDE subscription with predictable monthly caps.
- Developers who do not want to configure providers, MCP servers, permissions, or context-management practices.
- Buyers that need signed repository-level evidence rather than task-local checkpoints and dashboards.
