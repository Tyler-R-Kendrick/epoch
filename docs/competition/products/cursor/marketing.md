# Marketing

## Target Customers

- Professional developers who already live in VS Code-like editors and want AI coding to happen inside that workspace.
- Startups and product teams trying to turn natural-language tasks into reviewed code faster.
- Engineering teams evaluating agentic PR review, background agents, and repository rules without replacing GitHub or their CI system.

## Positioning

Cursor positions itself as the AI code editor: codebase-aware, natural-language-driven, and increasingly agentic. The marketing center is not "new version control"; it is faster software work in a familiar editor with deeper AI integration than extensions can provide.

## Customer Model

Cursor uses individual and team subscriptions, usage limits, model-cost accounting for agent usage, and add-ons such as Bugbot review. Team packaging emphasizes pooled usage, settings, and review workflows; enterprise adoption depends on privacy, retention, policy, and cost controls.

## Captures

- Developers who want a mainstream IDE experience with AI built in rather than bolted on.
- Teams willing to accept a proprietary editor in exchange for velocity, model access, and polished agent UX.
- Organizations that want AI review and code-generation loops before they redesign source-control semantics.

## Misses

- Teams that require self-hosted, forge-neutral, or cryptographically auditable change history.
- Developers who prefer terminal-first agents, open-source editors, or explicit Git-native workflows.
- Buyers who need predictable spend for long-running autonomous work.

## Epoch Lessons

Epoch should not compete with Cursor as an editor. It should make the history beneath any editor or agent trustworthy: signed intent, durable checkpoints, materialized versions, and review evidence that can outlive the UI that produced them.
