# Marketing

## Target Customer

Kiro targets developers and teams who like the speed of coding agents but want more explicit product and engineering structure before code changes land. It is especially relevant to AWS-oriented organizations, teams standardizing agent workflows, and developers who want an IDE, CLI, and web agent under one product umbrella.

## Positioning

- Kiro positions itself around "spec-driven development" and the move from vibe coding to production-ready workflows.
- The marketing emphasizes requirements, architecture, implementation tasks, hooks, steering, MCP, subagents, and advanced context management.
- Pricing copy presents Kiro as a paid productivity platform with free entry, individual tiers, team billing, analytics, SSO, and enterprise security controls.

## Customer Model

- Individual plans include a free tier and paid Pro, Pro+, and Power tiers with monthly credits.
- Paid plans add premium models and optional overages charged per credit.
- Team plans add centralized billing, usage analytics, SAML/SCIM SSO via AWS IAM Identity Center, organizational management, and enterprise controls.

## Who They Capture

- Teams worried about unstructured AI-generated code and missing requirements.
- Developers who want a VS Code-like environment with more explicit planning artifacts.
- AWS customers who prefer vendor-backed controls and billing over a startup-only coding agent.

## Who They Miss

- Developers who want one-shot fixes without spec overhead.
- Users sensitive to credit metering and overage anxiety.
- Organizations that cannot let a cloud-backed agent touch proprietary code, credentials, or local build systems without independent provenance.
