# Forge and community power user

## Who I am

I maintain repositories and contributor communities across mature forges and channel-first social products. GitHub and GitLab set my expectations for review and status; Forgejo, SourceHut, and Tangled shape my expectations for sovereignty; Discord, Slack, Telegram, X, and Bluesky shape how I scan and participate in communities.

- secondary jobs: migrate repositories, moderate channels, evaluate contributor trust, and recover from failed merges

## Goals

- See repository, review, identity, and community state in one coherent journey.
- Keep familiar forge affordances while gaining signed, portable Epoch history.
- Understand who acted, what changed, and what remains blocked without opening five tools.

## Constraints

- Existing contributor identity, URLs, and Git workflows cannot disappear during migration.
- Community conversation is not subordinate to one repository.
- Dense expert surfaces still need keyboard, mobile, and assistive-technology paths.

## Accessibility & inclusion needs

- Status and trust cues must be textual, not color-only or hover-only.
- Channel and review navigation must work by keyboard and remain legible at zoom.
- Identity language must not assume one forge, employer, locale, or social graph.

## Success looks like

- I can discover a project, discuss a change, verify provenance, review it, and return to the community context without losing my place.
- Git/forge migration feels reversible and signed Epoch state is clearer than incumbent audit trails.

## Failure modes I hate

- Repo-only navigation that erases community identity and channel context.
- A signed badge with no inspectable actor, event, or verification details.
- Familiar terms reused with different semantics and no migration explanation.
